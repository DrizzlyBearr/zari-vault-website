A South African passport takes a bit of planning, especially around busy travel seasons. Whether it is your first passport or a replacement for an expiring one, here is how the process works and how to avoid the common mistakes.

## First, the timing

- An adult passport is generally valid for **ten years**.
- South African passports are not "renewed" in the sense of extending an old one. When yours is expiring, you **apply for a new passport**.
- **Apply well before you travel.** Processing times vary, and leaving it late is the most common cause of a missed trip.

## Where to apply

- Online via the **eHomeAffairs** portal (ehome.dha.gov.za), then book an appointment, or
- At a **Department of Home Affairs office**, or
- At a **participating bank branch**, which many travellers find faster.

The typical flow is: apply and pay online, book a slot, then attend in person for your photograph and fingerprints.

## What to bring

- Your **Smart ID card** or green ID book
- Your **current or expired passport**, if you have one
- The **application fee** (fees change, so confirm the current amount)
- Photographs are usually taken at the office

## Applying for a child's passport

Children's passports have extra requirements, typically including the child's birth certificate and the **consent of both parents or legal guardians**. Because these rules are strict and travel with minors is closely checked, confirm the current requirements before your appointment.

## Validity rules to check before you travel

Having a passport is not always enough. Many destinations require that your passport:

- Is valid for at least **six months** beyond your travel dates, and
- Has a minimum number of **blank pages** for stamps and visas.

Always check the entry requirements of the country you are visiting, and confirm any visa you may need.
