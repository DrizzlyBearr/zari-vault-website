If you have ever opened a bank account, taken out a policy or signed up with a financial provider, you have been "FICA'd". It is one of the most common reasons South Africans get asked for documents, and getting it right the first time saves a frustrating second trip. Here is what FICA actually requires.

## What FICA is (in one sentence)

FICA is the **Financial Intelligence Centre Act**. It requires banks and other financial institutions to confirm **who you are** and **where you live** before they do business with you, as a measure against fraud and money laundering.

## The two things they need to verify

1. **Your identity**
2. **Your residential address**

That is the whole job. Everything on the document list below exists to prove one of those two things.

## Proof of identity

Any one of these usually works:

- South African **Smart ID card**
- Green **ID book**
- Valid **passport** (often used by non-citizens)

## Proof of residential address

This is where most people get caught out. You need a document that shows your **name and physical address**, usually dated within the **last three months**. Commonly accepted examples include:

- A **utility bill** (water, electricity, rates)
- A **municipal account**
- A **bank statement** from another bank
- A **retail or store account** statement
- A **lease or rental agreement**
- A **telephone or cellphone account**

Requirements vary slightly between institutions, so if in doubt, ask your provider which documents they accept.

## What if the bills are not in your name?

This is extremely common, especially if you live with family or rent informally. Most institutions accept:

- An **affidavit** confirming where you live, often together with
- The **account holder's** proof of address and a copy of their ID.

Ask your bank for their exact requirement, because the wording of the affidavit sometimes matters.

## A note on copies

For FICA, institutions generally need the **original or a certified copy**, not a phone photo. A digital copy is great for your own reference and for lower-stakes situations, but formal verification is stricter.
