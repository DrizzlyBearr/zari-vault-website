You have probably seen "POPIA compliant" on websites and consent forms without anyone explaining what it means. Here is the plain-language version, and why it matters to you as a South African.

## What POPIA is

POPIA is the **Protection of Personal Information Act**. It came into full effect in **July 2021**, and it does two things:

1. It gives **you** rights over your personal information.
2. It places **obligations** on any organisation that collects or uses your personal information.

"Personal information" is broad. It includes your name, ID number, contact details, address, financial information, biometrics and more.

## Your rights under POPIA

In plain terms, you have the right to:

- **Know** what personal information an organisation holds about you.
- **Access** that information and get a copy.
- **Correct** information that is wrong.
- **Have it deleted** when there is no good reason to keep it.
- **Object** to certain uses, such as direct marketing.
- **Complain** to the **Information Regulator** if an organisation mishandles your data.

## What organisations must do

Any business that holds your data (POPIA calls them "responsible parties") has to, among other things:

- Only collect information they actually need, for a clear purpose.
- Use it only for that purpose.
- Keep it **secure** and no longer than necessary.
- Tell you if a data breach puts your information at risk.

## What it means for everyday South Africans

The practical upshot is that your personal information is not a free-for-all. Companies need a lawful reason to collect it, have to protect it, and have to answer to you and to the regulator if they do not. If a company is spamming you or holding data it should not, POPIA is the framework that lets you push back.
